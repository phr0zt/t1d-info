A massage table is one of the few places where someone else's hands spend an hour near your sensor and your infusion set. This page covers what can go wrong with devices during a session, and the short list of reasons to move the appointment.

## Your sensor and pump on the table

:::warning[Compression lows]
Pressure on a CGM sensor — lying on it, or a hand pressing on it — can show a sudden low that isn't real. Confirm a surprising number with a finger prick before acting on it.
:::

In a study where volunteers wore several sensors at once, sudden false readings were strongly linked with lying on the sensors; most of them were drops. The community calls these "compression lows"; the research literature calls them pressure-induced sensor attenuation. Every manufacturer's manual says the same thing: when a reading doesn't match how you feel, check with a meter.

The other direction matters just as much: don't dismiss a real low because you assume it's compression. If in doubt, prick.

**Oils and lotions.** Dexcom and Abbott both warn against putting lotion near the sensor, because it stops the adhesive holding. Massage oil is the same problem. Neither company says this about pump infusion sets specifically, but the same logic applies. Ask the therapist to leave a hand's width of clear, oil-free skin around each device.

**Show where they are.** Point out your sensor and infusion set before you lie down. Draping, stretching and friction near them are how they get pulled off.

**Pumps.** Keep the pump where you can reach it. If you disconnect for the session, agree with your diabetes team how long is fine — and reconnect before you leave.

## When to move the appointment

A short list of situations calls for rescheduling:

- **Ketones raised.** Blood ketones of 0.6 or above, or high blood sugar while feeling unwell. That's your [sick-day plan](/en/emergencies/sick-days/), not a massage.
- **A severe low in the last 24 hours** — one that needed someone else's help. Your defences are blunted for the next one.
- **Wounds or infection** in the area to be treated — an open sore, ulcer, blister or infection, especially on the feet.
- **Fever or acute illness.** Sick days come first. Book again once you are back to your usual numbers.

The FQM lists poorly managed diabetes, hypoglycemia episodes and ulcers or wounds as reasons not to massage. Postponing for ketones or a recent severe low is clinical common sense rather than a published massage rule.

:::do[Everything else is a reason to talk, not to stay home]
Newly diagnosed, pump or injections, existing complications — none of these is a reason to skip massage. They are reasons to tell the therapist.
:::

Some advice circulating online says to get a doctor's clearance before every booking. The FQM suggests checking with your doctor only when in doubt — for example, with circulation problems or complications you are unsure about. Most people with type 1 don't need permission to get a massage.
