This section is for people with type 1 diabetes who are thinking about massage therapy, orthotherapy or other hands-on care — and for the massage therapists and orthotherapists who treat them.

Start with the reassurance: most people with type 1 can book a massage like anyone else. What changes is a short list of practical things. Massage, heat and working muscles can make a recent insulin dose act faster. A CGM sensor can read falsely low when it is pressed on, and oil stops its adhesive holding. Numb feet can't tell a therapist when the pressure or the heat is too much. And in Québec, choosing a therapist works differently from the rest of Canada, because neither massage therapy nor orthotherapy is a regulated profession here.

The pages below cover who does what and what massage can honestly claim, insulin and lows on the table, sensors and pumps, what changes with complications, what to tell the therapist, and a page written for therapists themselves. There is also a [printable card](/downloads/en/krkt-t1de-07-therapist-card-en.pdf) to hand over at the first visit.

:::note
This is education, not medical advice. Where to inject, when, and how long to keep an area clear belong with your diabetes team.
:::
