This is the part of massage that is genuinely different with type 1. Rubbing, heating or working the muscle under a recent injection gets insulin into the blood faster — and a session is also a setting where a low is easy to miss. This page explains both, and what to do about them.

## Insulin moves faster under a working hand

This effect has been measured since the late 1970s.

:::stats
- **~6×** faster insulin absorption when an injection site was massaged for 30 minutes — and blood sugar fell faster too
- **110%** more absorption after two sauna sessions, with blood sugar 3.0 to 3.3 mmol/L lower
- **135%** more absorption in the first ten minutes of leg exercise after a leg injection
:::

Hot stones, heat packs and hot tubs work the same way as a sauna: heat speeds absorption.

These were small studies — nine volunteers in the main massage study — using older human insulin. Nobody has measured massage over a rapid-acting analogue or a pump site directly. Studies of local heat on pump sites with rapid-acting insulin point in the same direction, so the safe assumption is that the effect applies.

FIT Canada, the Canadian injection technique guidance, says massaging the injection site immediately before or after an injection is not recommended, because it makes insulin action unpredictable.

:::do[What to do]
- Don't inject into an area that is about to be worked on.
- Ask the therapist to skip recent injection sites and your pump site.
- No heat — stones, packs, hot towels — over those areas.
:::

How long to keep an area clear depends on the insulin. Your diabetes team can tell you how long yours stays active.

## Lows on the table

Lying still, relaxed, eyes closed, face in a cradle: the early signs of a low are easy to miss, or to mistake for relaxation. Diabète Québec warns that blood sugar can drop unexpectedly during massage. No study has looked at lows during massage directly, so what follows is precaution rather than data.

**Before.** Check your glucose. If you are low or heading down, treat it and wait, or move the appointment. Eat as usual — don't arrive on an empty stomach.

**Within reach.** Keep fast sugar on the edge of the table or in your hand, not in a bag across the room. Agree on one word with the therapist that means "stop, I need sugar". Many people with type 1 won't interrupt a session they are paying for; this gives you permission in advance.

**After.** Check again before you drive. The Fédération québécoise des massothérapeutes (FQM) suggests checking before and after at least the first two or three sessions, so you learn how your own numbers respond.

:::emergency[If a low happens during a session]
- Stop. Sit up slowly.
- Take 15 g of fast-acting sugar, and recheck in 15 minutes.
- If the person is unconscious or having a seizure, call 911 and give nothing by mouth.
:::

See [treating a low](/en/emergencies/treating-a-low/) for what 15 g looks like.

## Start simple

If you are new to massage with type 1, start with a relaxation massage rather than deep work, and write down your glucose before and after, along with your last dose and where you gave it. Two or three sessions of notes will show your own pattern — which is worth more than any general rule.
