Most of the safety in a massage with type 1 comes from two minutes of conversation before you lie down. This page gives you the words, and explains how to choose a therapist in Québec, where the title alone tells you very little.

## What to tell the therapist

Say this, or hand over the [printable therapist card (PDF)](/downloads/en/krkt-t1de-07-therapist-card-en.pdf) — two copies on one letter page, with blanks to fill in:

1. I have type 1 diabetes and use insulin — a pump / injections.
2. My sensor is **here**, and my pump site is **here**. No oil, pressure or stretching on them, please.
3. My last insulin was at ___, in my ___. Please skip that area, and no heat there.
4. My glucose right now is ___, and it is steady / rising / falling.
5. If I say "**sugar**", stop — I may be going low.
6. Numb areas: ___. Broad pressure there, and no heat. Also: stiff joints, wounds, or dizziness when I stand up.

**Bring** your meter or phone, and fast sugar within reach of the table.

People often skip this at the clinic because it feels like a lot to explain to a stranger. Saying it once at home makes it easier — and the card does the explaining for you.

## Choosing a therapist in Québec

**Check membership.** Because massage therapy and orthotherapy aren't regulated in Québec, the title isn't protected: anyone can use it. The association a therapist belongs to is the real check. The Fédération québécoise des massothérapeutes (FQM), Mon Réseau Plus (RMPQ) and RITMA all publish member directories. Complaints go to the therapist's association rather than to a professional order.

**One question to ask.** "Have you worked with clients who use insulin, a pump or a sensor?" A good answer sounds a lot like the [page for therapists](/en/manual-therapy/for-therapists/).

## Insurance, coverage and taxes

**Group insurance receipts.** Group plans usually require a receipt from a member in good standing of an association that your insurer recognises. The receipt shows the therapist's association and membership number. Some plans also want a doctor's recommendation. Check your insurer's list of recognised associations before you book, not after.

**RAMQ.** The public plan doesn't cover massage therapy or orthotherapy.

**Taxes.** In Québec, massage therapy doesn't count for the federal medical expense tax credit — the Canada Revenue Agency recognises massage therapists only in some other provinces. It is generally not eligible for the Québec credit either. Physiotherapy is eligible.

:::note
We don't recommend specific clinics or associations. The point is that, in Québec, the patient does the checking that a professional order would do elsewhere.
:::

## Where to get help

- **Diabetes Canada:** 1-800-BANTING (1-800-226-8464)
- **Info-Santé:** 811 — a nurse, 24/7, free
- **Emergency:** 911 — unconscious, having a seizure, or won't wake

Every number on these pages is general guidance, not your plan. Take the pattern you record — glucose before and after, last dose and site — to your diabetes team.
