Some foods feel like obvious choices for a low because they are sweet, but they work slowly or not at all. This page explains which ones and why. It also covers the honest exception: when they're all there is.

## What slows sugar down

Sugar raises blood glucose once it leaves the stomach and is absorbed. Fat and protein keep food in the stomach longer, so the sugar arrives later. Fructose, the main sugar in fruit, has to be converted by the liver first. Anything with a lot of either is slower than glucose or table sugar.

:::warning[Not first choices]
- **Chocolate** — bars, chocolate chips, and **Canadian Smarties**. Fat slows the sugar. ISPAD says to avoid fat-containing foods such as chocolate as the first treatment.
- **Cookies, cake, pastries, granola bars** — fat, and often fibre.
- **Milk and ice cream** — fat and protein. Diabetes Canada lists milk among the slower options.
- **Diet, zero and "no sugar added" drinks** — no sugar at all. They do nothing for a low.
- **Glucose gel** — less than 1.0 mmol/L at 20 minutes, according to Diabetes Canada. Tablets are better.
- **Whole fruit and cereal** — fibre and fructose, so they're slow.
:::

## The diet-drink mix-up

This is the classic mistake at a family table, a party or a corner store. The regular and diet cans sit side by side and look almost identical. If you keep pop in the house to treat lows, keep it clearly apart from the diet version, or keep juice boxes or tablets instead. A helper who grabs a can for someone else should read it first.

## The overtreating trap

Slow foods do a second kind of harm. While you wait for them to work, the low keeps going, and so does the hunger. Someone who starts with a cookie often ends up eating half the box before they feel better, then runs high a couple of hours later. A measured dose of something fast avoids both problems.

## If it's all you have

:::do[Slow sugar beats no sugar]
If chocolate, a granola bar or a glass of milk is the only thing within reach, eat it. A slow treatment is far better than none. Then check again in 15 minutes, as always, and restock your proper kit afterwards.
:::

Juice and honey are on the chart because they work and they're easy to find. They are simply a little slower than glucose or table sugar. When you have the choice, pick tablets, Rockets, sugar water or regular pop.
