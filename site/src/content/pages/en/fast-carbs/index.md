When blood sugar drops under 4.0 mmol/L, the fix is sugar, fast, and a measured amount of it. This section is the practical companion to [treating a low](/en/emergencies/treating-a-low/). It covers what to grab, how much, and what to leave on the shelf.

It started from a chart many people share: Beyond Type 1's list of fast-acting carbs, built from US brands. A US chart doesn't travel well. In Canada, **Smarties are chocolate**: the dextrose candy on the American list is sold here as Rockets. Piece counts differ between US and Canadian bags. Some US products don't exist on Québec shelves at all. So we rebuilt it with Canadian labels, Health Canada's nutrient data, and the amounts Diabetes Canada and Diabète Québec actually give.

The pages below explain why some sugars work faster than others and who needs a different amount, then give the chart itself with a method for adding your own candy. After that comes what isn't a first choice, and how to treat a low like medicine so it doesn't turn into a high.

There is also a [printable fridge chart](/downloads/en/krkt-t1de-08-fast-carbs-chart-en.pdf) — two sides, Letter size — with every amount on this page.

:::note
This is education, not medical advice. Your own amount, and whether you use less on an automated pump, belongs with your diabetes team.
:::
