These are amounts that give about 15 g of carbohydrate, the standard treatment for a low in an adult. They come from Canadian labels where we could find them, from Health Canada's nutrient data, and from the lists Diabète Québec and Diabetes Canada publish. Recipes and piece sizes change, so if your package says something different, the label wins.

## From the pantry

| Food | Amount | Carbs |
|---|---|---|
| **Dex4 glucose tablets** (fastest) | 4 tablets | 16 g |
| White sugar, dissolved in water | 15 mL (1 tbsp) | 13–15 g |
| Sugar packets (Canadian, 3.5 g) | 4 packets | 14 g |
| Maple syrup | 15 mL (1 tbsp) | 14 g |
| Corn syrup | 15 mL (1 tbsp) | about 15 g |
| Honey (a bit slower) | 15 mL (1 tbsp) | 18 g |
| Regular pop, not diet | 150 mL (⅔ cup) | 16 g |
| Fruit juice (a bit slower) | 150 mL (⅔ cup) | 18 g |

Diabetes Canada counts 15 mL of sugar as 15 g. By weight, Health Canada's data puts it closer to 13 g. Diabetes Canada also lists 5 sugar cubes, but cube sizes vary. Maple syrup is the Québec answer: it is almost pure sucrose, and it is probably already in the fridge.

## From the candy aisle

| Candy | Amount | Carbs |
|---|---|---|
| **Rockets** (the US "Smarties"; fastest) | 2 rolls | 14 g |
| Life Savers, hard | 4 to 6 — check the roll | 15 g |
| Jelly Belly jelly beans | 15 beans | 16 g |
| Skittles (a little fat) | 15 pieces | 14 g |
| Starburst (a little fat) | 4 pieces | 16 g |
| Sour Patch Kids, Canadian bag | 7 pieces | 15 g |

Rockets are pressed dextrose: the same sugar as glucose tablets, in a roll that fits in a pocket. Diabète Québec lists 2 rolls. Diabetes Canada's list counts 6 Life Savers at 2.5 g each, but the current US label works out to about 3.75 g a piece, so look at your roll.

## Same name, different candy

:::warning[Smarties are chocolate in Canada]
Canadian Smarties are candy-coated chocolate, and the fat slows them down. The pressed-dextrose candy that US charts call Smarties is sold in Canada as **Rockets**. If you copied a list from an American website, check this one first.
:::

Three more differences from the US chart:

- **Pieces aren't pieces.** Beyond Type 1's chart says 5 Sour Patch Kids make 15 g (its web text says 6 make 14 g). On the Canadian bag, 16 pieces are 36 g, so 5 is only about 11 g. You need 7.
- **Some rows don't exist here.** Gushers, Brach's jelly beans, some Welch's pouch sizes and candy corn are US products or US pack sizes. We left them out rather than guess.
- **Chews contain fat.** Skittles and Starburst contain palm kernel oil. They still work, just a little slower than jelly beans or Rockets.

## Make your own row

Any candy you like can join the chart. All you need is the Nutrition Facts table on the package.

1. **Read Carbohydrate.** Find the serving size (say, 16 pieces) and the Carbohydrate line (say, 36 g). Use the Carbohydrate total, not the Sugars line.
2. **Divide.** 36 g ÷ 16 pieces = 2.25 g per piece.
3. **Count to 15.** 15 ÷ 2.25 = 6.7. Round up to 7 pieces. Write the number on the bag in marker.

:::do[Look at the fat line too]
The fastest candies have 0 g of fat: hard candy, jelly beans, most gummies, Rockets. If the Fat line isn't zero, the candy will be slower.
:::
