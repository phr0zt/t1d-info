Losing the warning signs — and getting them back

Repeated lows train the body to stop reacting to them. The shaking and pounding heart that used to arrive at 3.5 stop firing, and the first sign of a low becomes confusion — or nothing at all.

This is a complication in its own right, and the one that most constrains everything else. It makes tight targets unsafe and it drives the fear that shapes daily decisions.

It is reversible

Strictly avoiding lows for anywhere from a couple of days to three months has been shown to bring the warning symptoms back, at least partly.

Diabetes Canada’s advice is to deliberately loosen targets for up to three months to do it.

So if someone is told to run higher for a while, that is good medicine. It is not backsliding, and nobody should treat it as such.

Recovery is often partial rather than complete — sensors restore some symptoms without fully restoring the hormone response. Sources: Diabetes Canada CPG Ch.14 · NIDDK workshop, Diabetes Care 2022.

The deliberate-loosening point is a powerful antidote to the idea that lower is always better and anything else is failure.
