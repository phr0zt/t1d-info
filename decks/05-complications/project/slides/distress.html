Diabetes distress is a normal reaction, not a weakness

1 in 4

to as many as 2 in 5 adults with type 1 report high levels of diabetes distress — the emotional weight of relentless daily management.

It is described in the literature as an expected response to living with diabetes. It is not a mental health disorder.

And here is what it is about

The most frequently reported source of that distress is worrying about the future and the possibility of serious complications — the exact subject of this deck.

It travels with worse numbers

Distress is associated with higher A1C and with doing less of the daily management — roughly as strongly as depression is. Adding fear does not improve either.

Good numbers don’t guarantee peace either

People can reach target through intense, unsustainable effort that costs them everywhere else. An A1C tells you nothing about what it took.

Burnout — exhaustion and detachment from the whole thing — is a related and common state. Both are worth saying out loud to your team. Sources: MILES-2 · ADA/NDSS Diabetes and Emotional Health.

If someone in the room recognises themselves here, that is the outcome. Distress is treatable and routinely goes unmentioned in appointments.
