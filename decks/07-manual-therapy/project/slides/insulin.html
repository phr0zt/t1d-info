Insulin moves faster under a working hand

Rubbing, heating or working the muscle under a recent injection gets insulin into the blood faster. This has been measured since the late 1970s.

~6×

faster insulin absorption when an injection site was massaged for 30 minutes — and blood sugar fell faster too.

9 volunteers, older human insulin (Linde 1986).

Heat does it too

Two sauna sessions raised absorption by 110% and left blood sugar 3.0 to 3.3 mmol/L lower. Hot stones, heat packs and hot tubs work the same way.

So does the muscle underneath

Leg exercise after a leg injection raised absorption by 135% in the first ten minutes.

What to do

Don’t inject into an area about to be worked on. Ask the therapist to skip recent injection sites and your pump site, and no heat there.

Sources: Linde 1986 · Koivisto 1980 · Koivisto & Felig 1978 · FIT Canada 2020. All small studies on older insulins; for rapid-acting analogues and pump sites the effect is inferred from heat studies (Raz 2009, Landau 2014).

FIT Canada: massaging the injection site immediately before or after an injection is not recommended, because it makes insulin action unpredictable. How long to keep clear depends on the insulin; your team can tell you how long yours stays active.
