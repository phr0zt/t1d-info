Your sensor and pump on the table

Compression lows

Pressure on a CGM sensor — lying on it, or a hand on it — can show a sudden low that isn’t real. Confirm a surprising number with a finger prick before acting on it.

Oils and lotions

Dexcom and Abbott both warn against lotion near the sensor: the adhesive stops holding. The same logic applies to pump sites. Ask for a hand’s width of clear, oil-free skin.

Show where they are

Point out your sensor and infusion set before you lie down. Draping, stretching and friction near them are how they get pulled off.

Pumps

Keep the pump where you can reach it. If you disconnect for the session, agree with your team how long is fine — and reconnect before you leave.

Sources: Mensh et al., J Diabetes Sci Technol 2013 · Dexcom G7 user guide · Abbott FreeStyle Libre 2 adhesion guide · Medtronic Guardian 4 user guide. Oils on pump sets: our extension of the lotion warnings.

“Compression low” is the community name; the literature calls it pressure-induced sensor attenuation. Every manual says to check with a meter whenever the reading doesn’t match how you feel.
