Why people with type 1 end up on the table

66%

of 1,217 adults in the DCCT/EDIC study — on average 31 years with type 1 — had at least one hand or shoulder condition.

These people were diagnosed decades ago; how common this is with today’s care isn’t known yet.

Frozen shoulder — 31%

A painful shoulder that slowly stiffens. About five times more likely with diabetes than without.

Carpal tunnel — 30%

Numb, tingling fingers, often at night.

Trigger finger — 28%

A finger that catches or locks when it bends.

Stiff hands — 22%

Can’t press the palms flat together (the “prayer sign”). Dupuytren’s contracture: 9%.

Sources: Larkin et al., Diabetes Care 2014 (DCCT/EDIC) · Zreik et al. 2016. Leading explanation: glucose slowly stiffening collagen (glycation) — years of exposure, not a verdict on effort.

These are common, under-recognised and treatable; many people assume a stiff shoulder is just age. Intensive versus conventional treatment in the original trial: 64% versus 68%, not a significant difference. This is not a scorecard.
