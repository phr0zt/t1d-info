Lumps, skin and getting up

Lumps where insulin goes

Rubbery lumps under the skin at injection or pump sites (lipohypertrophy) affect about a third of people with type 1. A therapist may notice them first. Insulin in those spots acts unpredictably — tell your team.

Skin

Avoid any wound, sore, blister or infection. Moisturising dry feet is fine — just not between the toes.

Getting up

Some people with long-standing type 1 get light-headed standing up. Sit on the edge of the table for a moment, then stand slowly.

Eyes

Recent eye surgery or advanced retinopathy? Ask your eye specialist about time spent face-down.

Sources: Deng et al., J Diabetes Investig 2018 (26 studies) · Diabetes Canada foot care chapter 2018 · FQM 2007 intake questions.

Lipohypertrophy is a genuinely useful thing for a therapist to spot, and it is a site-rotation issue, not a personal failing.
