What massage can honestly claim

Reasonably supported

Feeling calmer. A meta-analysis of 37 trials found single sessions lowered anxiety, blood pressure and heart rate.

For frozen shoulder in diabetes, joint mobilisation plus exercise had the largest effect across 8 trials — though the evidence is low quality.

Disputed

“Lowers stress hormones.” One review reported cortisol falling by about a third; a stricter meta-analysis found the effect indistinguishable from zero.

And stress itself moves blood sugar differently in different people — up, down, or not at all.

Not shown

Treating diabetes. Small studies saw glucose fall after sessions, most likely because massage speeds insulin absorption — a reason to check, not a therapy.

Preventing complications by “improving circulation”. No good evidence, and no guideline lists it.

Sources: Moyer 2004 and 2011 · Field 2005 · Alsubheen 2019 · Ezzo 2001 · Sajedi 2011 · Wiesli 2005. Evidence for every glucose claim is low: small, short, mostly uncontrolled studies.

This slide exists because the claims circulating online — lowers cortisol, improves circulation, helps manage glucose — are stronger than the research. Say it plainly: go because it feels good and helps you move, not to treat diabetes.
