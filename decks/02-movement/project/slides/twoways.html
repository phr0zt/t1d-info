A jog and a sprint do opposite things

This is the part nobody explains, and it’s why the same person gets two completely different results from two workouts and assumes they did something wrong.

Steady aerobic → down

Jogging, cycling, swimming laps, a long walk, most team-sport running.

Muscles pull glucose out of the blood, and the insulin you injected doesn’t switch off the way a working pancreas would. Blood sugar falls, often within about 45 minutes.

Hard and short → up

Sprints, heavy lifting, intervals, a match, anything near flat-out.

Adrenaline and other stress hormones tell the liver to dump glucose faster than the muscles can use it. Blood sugar rises — and that is a normal response, not a mistake.

Real sessions are usually a mix, so the honest answer is often “both, in that order”. What matters is knowing which one you are about to do.
