Heat, cold, altitude and water

Heat speeds insulin up

Warm skin absorbs insulin faster, and the heat itself burns more energy. Glucose falls quicker than you expect. Dehydration also makes sensor readings less reliable, so drink steadily.

Cold slows it down

And it breaks meters. A glucose meter kept below freezing may refuse to read or read wrongly — a sensor is the better choice for winter sport.

Altitude

Meters can read inaccurately high up, appetite drops, and energy use rises. Thin air also makes for poor decisions — plan on the ground.

Water

You cannot treat a low underwater, and nobody can see you having one. Never swim alone, never after any recent low, and never without someone on the side who knows.

Diving with type 1 needs its own specialist protocol and is not something to improvise. Sources: ISPAD 2022 · ADA 2016.

Alpine skiing, climbing, trekking alone and open water are the four settings the consensus statement singles out as unsafe after any recent low.
