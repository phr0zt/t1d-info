Tricks worth trying, honestly graded

Weights before cardio

Doing the resistance work first blunts the fall during the aerobic part that follows.

Weak evidence. Diabetes Canada grades this at its lowest level — essentially one small study. Free to try, not a rule.

A ten-second sprint

All-out for ten seconds at the start, during, or at the end of a session may hold glucose up.

Weak evidence, same lowest grade. And after already-intense exercise it may push you higher instead.

Interval training

Short bursts of hard work mixed into easier stretches. Diabetes Canada recommends it specifically to reduce the risk of going low during exercise.

Better evidence than the other two — though some studies link it to more overnight lows.

Source: Diabetes Canada Recommendations 2 and 7. Grades are stated because a low-grade recommendation is worth trying but not worth trusting.

Showing the evidence grade openly is unusual in patient material and tends to increase trust rather than reduce it.
