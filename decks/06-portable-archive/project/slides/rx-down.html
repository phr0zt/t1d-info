Medicines that push it down, or hide the fall

Alcohol, including in medicine

Your liver stops releasing glucose while it clears alcohol. Some liquid medicines and syrups contain it — read the label.

Some antibiotics

The fluoroquinolones — levofloxacin, moxifloxacin, ciprofloxacin — can move glucose in either direction, sometimes sharply.

High-dose salicylates and quinine

Both are recognised causes of lows. Uncommon, but they turn up in older prescriptions and travel kits.

Beta blockers — the hiding one

They barely move the number. What they take away is your ability to feel it move. Next slide.

Anything that causes lows is more dangerous in combination with insulin than it would be for anyone else taking it.

Fluoroquinolones are the practical one here. They are prescribed often and the dysglycemia warning rarely gets mentioned at the counter.
