For family, friends and caretakers

Worth knowing

  • Where the fast sugar is kept
  • Where the glucagon is, and which kind
  • That sweating and confusion can mean a low
  • To say “they have type 1 diabetes” to any paramedic
  • That a new steroid or antibiotic may wreck their week

Worth not doing

  • Commenting on what they eat
  • Reading their numbers back at them
  • Moving or rationing their supplies
  • Treating a bad mood as a bad reading out loud
  • Changing meal times without telling them

Better phrasing

“Have you checked?” lands better than “you look low”.

“What do you need?” beats guessing and handing them the wrong thing.

“I’m eating at seven” is more useful than “are you allowed this?”

This is a condition managed roughly a hundred and eighty times a week, for life. Surveillance wears people down faster than the illness does.

If caretakers take one thing away, make it the middle column. Good intentions cause most of what is listed there.
