The question was actually settled, in a trial, forty years ago

From 1983 to 1993, the DCCT followed 1,441 people with type 1 diabetes, split into two groups.

One group got the standard care of the day. The other got intensive management — more injections, far more monitoring, constant adjustment.

After six and a half years, the difference between the groups came down to one thing: an average A1C of about 7% against about 9%.

Two percentage points

That gap is the entire experiment. Everything on the next slide is what those two points were worth.

It is also the most important thing anyone with type 1 has ever been given: proof that the daily effort is buying something real.

Source: Diabetes Control and Complications Trial, New England Journal of Medicine 1993

Emphasise that this was a randomised trial, not an observation. It is the reason the whole care model exists.
