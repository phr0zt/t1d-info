What each part of a meal actually does

Carbohydrate

Fast and large. Up within 15 to 30 minutes, peaking around an hour or two.

This is the one you dose for. Total carbohydrate has the biggest effect on blood sugar of anything on the plate.

Protein

Slow and modest. A meaningful rise only when there’s a lot of it, arriving roughly three to five hours later.

A normal portion alongside carbs needs no separate dose.

Fat

Doesn’t raise blood sugar directly. It slows the whole meal down and makes insulin work less well for hours.

Which is why the late rise catches people out. Two slides from now.

Fibre

Counted as carbohydrate on the label, but your body doesn’t absorb it, so you subtract it back out.

It also slows everything around it down, which flattens the peak.

Sources: Diabetes Canada clinical practice guidelines · ISPAD 2022 nutrition guidelines · Diabetes UK

Four cards, left to right, fastest to slowest. That ordering is the mental model the rest of the deck builds on.
