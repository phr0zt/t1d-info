Glucagon: the medicine somebody else gives you

It reverses a severe low when the person cannot swallow. Which means the patient is never the one who uses it — so this slide is for everyone else in the room.

Nasal, 3 mg

A puff into one nostril. No needle, no mixing, and it works even if they are unconscious — they do not need to breathe it in.

Injectable, 1 mg

Into the thigh, through clothing if you have to. Newer pens come ready to use instead of needing mixing.

Then, every time

Call 911. Roll them onto their side — vomiting is common afterwards. Stay until they are properly awake and have eaten.

After drinking, it works less well

Glucagon tells the liver to release glucose. Alcohol stops the liver doing that. Past two drinks, call the ambulance rather than wait.

Everyone on insulin should have one, in date, somewhere the people around them can find it — not buried in a drawer they don’t know about.

Ask the room, honestly: who knows where their person's glucagon is right now? The usual answer is the point of the slide.
