It is described in the literature as an expected response to living with this, not a mental health disorder.

What it is mostly about

The most commonly reported source is worrying about the future and about complications. The fear is already there; nobody needs to add to it.

Burnout is real too

Exhaustion and detachment from the whole thing. Depression runs at roughly three times the general rate in type 1.

And a specific risk worth naming

Around a quarter of people on insulin screen positive for disordered eating, and about one in five report reducing insulin, often to influence weight. It damages eyes and kidneys faster than other weight-control behaviours, and it is common, treatable and nothing to be ashamed of.

All of this is worth raising with your team the way you would raise a blood result. Sources: MILES-2 · ADA/NDSS · Eating Behaviors 2024.

Handle gently. If someone recognises themselves, the next step is a clinician — offer to help find one rather than pressing for detail.
