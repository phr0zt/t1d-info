The job is replacing a feedback loop, by hand, forever

A working pancreas measures blood sugar and adjusts insulin continuously, hundreds of times an hour, with no conscious effort at all.

Replacing that means estimating what is in your food, predicting what your body is about to do, injecting a decision that cannot be taken back, and then living with the result for the next four hours. Several times a day. Every day. With no days off.

Everything else in this deck is detail. This is the actual work, and it is far harder than it looks from outside.

For family members in the room, this is the slide that conveys the scale. The decision-cannot-be-taken-back point is what separates insulin from most medication.
