The sentences that do damage

Never

  • “You’ll go blind / lose a foot / end up on dialysis”
  • “My grandmother had diabetes and she...”
  • “Is your sugar good today?”
  • “Should you be eating that?”
  • “Are you allowed that?”
  • “You just need more discipline”

Instead

  • “How are you doing with it?”
  • “What would actually help right now?”
  • “That looks like a lot of work.”
  • “Tell me a bit more about what worries you.”
  • Or nothing at all about the number on the screen.

Why it matters

Judgement causes concealment. People under-report readings they expect to be judged for — and hidden numbers are a safety problem, not just a feelings problem.

Across surveys, 96% of people with diabetes reject “sufferer” and 83% reject “good” and “bad” applied to blood sugar.

The left column comes verbatim from published guidance, not from opinion. Sources: dStigmatize · ADA 2017 · Diabetes Canada Language Matters · Diabetes Australia.

Saying these are published avoid-lists stops the slide being heard as a personal rebuke to someone in the room.
