Your body remembers the good stretches too

Researchers call it metabolic memory. A period of near-normal glucose keeps paying off for years afterwards — even if the numbers later rise again.

In the four to eight years after the trial ended, when both groups had the same A1C, the original intensive group still had 72% less eye disease progression and 84% less new kidney protein leakage.

And it cuts the other way

The effect fades after about ten years. Researchers call that metabolic amnesia.

Which means a difficult year, a rough adolescence or a period when life made diabetes impossible is not a permanent sentence. The body forgets in both directions.

Source: Understanding Metabolic Memory, Diabetes Care 2021 · EDIC. Say both halves — this is never “the damage is already done”.

People who have had a bad stretch often conclude their future is already fixed. The attenuation evidence says otherwise, and it is the most useful thing you can tell them.
