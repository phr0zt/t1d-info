Legal doesn’t mean mild

10–30%

of people who use cannabis develop a cannabis use disorder, according to Canada’s Lower-Risk Cannabis Use Guidelines.

Starting young, using daily, and high-THC products each push you toward the top of that range.

Potency is the variable that matters

Higher THC is strongly linked to more problems — mental health, dependence, injuries. Concentrates, carts and shatter are in a different league from the flower most people picture.

Daily use is the red line

Daily or near-daily use is the single strongest predictor of long-term harm. Occasional — a weekend, not a routine — is a genuinely different risk.

And for your diabetes specifically

Every day of munchies and missed doses shows up in your A1C. It’s the months of running high, not any single session, that damages eyes, kidneys and nerves.

Source: Canada’s Lower-Risk Cannabis Use Guidelines — canada.ca

The occasional-versus-daily distinction is the practical takeaway. It gives a realistic target instead of an abstinence message that gets ignored.
