The risk a deck like this one can create

Around a quarter of people living with insulin-treated diabetes screen positive for disordered eating, and roughly one in five report skipping or reducing insulin, often to influence weight.

It is a foreseeable consequence of a condition where the medication that keeps you alive also affects your weight, and where numbers are discussed as if they were grades.

Reducing insulin leads to eye and kidney damage more often than other weight-control behaviours do, and it raises the risk of ketoacidosis.

Why it is in this deck

Telling someone that high glucose destroys their eyes, while insulin is also the thing that adds weight, builds exactly the thinking that leads to insulin restriction.

A complications talk that ignores this is not neutral. If any of it sounds familiar, it is a conversation with a clinician, not something to manage alone — and it is common, treatable and not shameful.

These are screening-questionnaire results rather than diagnoses. Sources: systematic review and meta-analysis, Eating Behaviors 2024.

This is a sensitive topic. If anyone recognises themselves, the right next step is a clinician, and support is available — offer to help find it rather than pressing for detail.
