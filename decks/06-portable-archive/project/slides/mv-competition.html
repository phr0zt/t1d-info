The pre-game high is nerves, not a mistake

Young athletes routinely run high before a match even when the same activity in training sends them low. Adrenaline tells the liver to release glucose and makes insulin work less well — and standing on the touchline waiting is exactly when adrenaline peaks.

It usually settles once play starts, on its own.

Correcting it aggressively is what produces the crash later. The nerves wear off, the insulin doesn’t.

What helps instead

  • Arrive early, so there is time to see it coming
  • Check earlier than you think you need to
  • Don’t over-fuel before the start
  • A gentle warm-up jog helps burn off the hormone response
  • On a pump, a small temporary basal increase — back to normal before kickoff

Source: ISPAD 2022, competition day guidance. Mild highs do not measurably hurt performance — a low very much does.

Coaches who understand this one slide stop pushing for a correction on the sideline, which is where the harm usually begins.
