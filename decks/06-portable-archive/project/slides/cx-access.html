Why care goes wrong, in Canada, with public healthcare

$18,306

a year

is what out-of-pocket costs can reach for someone with type 1 in parts of Canada, according to Diabetes Canada.

Over half

face costs above 3% of family income

or cannot follow the treatment their doctor recommended. That is a national figure, not a foreign one.

In Québec specifically

The public insulin pump programme covers under-18s only. An adult diagnosed at 25 gets no public pump funding — whether they use one depends on private insurance, not on clinical need.

And the sensor paradox

RAMQ covers glucose sensors for adults only if their A1C is off target, or they have frequent or unrecognised lows — so doing well can disqualify you from the tool that helped.

Add depression at roughly three times the general rate, and it becomes clear why “improper care” is usually a description of circumstances. Sources: Diabetes Canada out-of-pocket costs report · Québec Insulin Pump Access Program · RAMQ via Diabète Québec.
