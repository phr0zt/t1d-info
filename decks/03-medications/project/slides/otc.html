The pharmacy aisle, where nobody asks you anything

Syrups carry carbs

Cough and cold liquids are often sugar-based, and the dose is repeated every four hours all day. Sugar-free versions exist — ask for one.

“Non-drowsy” means a decongestant

That is usually pseudoephedrine, which pushes glucose up and heart rate with it. It hides inside combination products.

NSAIDs on a sick day

Ibuprofen and naproxen are on the hold list when you’re dehydrated. Acetaminophen is usually the safer choice for fever — see the next slide.

“Blood sugar support” supplements

Not standardised, not tested against insulin, and some genuinely lower glucose — which is how they cause lows on top of your doses.

Simplest habit in the whole deck: carry the box to the counter and ask. It takes a minute and it is free.

Supplements are the one people conceal, because they do not think of them as drugs. Ask about them by name.
