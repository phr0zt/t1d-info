Sick days: what keeps going, what pauses

A sick day means vomiting, diarrhea, a fever, or not managing to eat and drink properly for about a day. Illness raises your insulin needs even when you’re eating nothing.

Keeps going

  • Your long-acting or basal insulin — always
  • Glucose checks every two to four hours, through the night too
  • Ketone checks, whatever the glucose says
  • Fluids, steadily, in small amounts
  • Correction doses as your plan sets out

Usually pauses while you’re dehydrated

  • SGLT2 inhibitors
  • ACE inhibitors and ARBs
  • Diuretics (water pills)
  • Metformin
  • NSAIDs — ibuprofen, naproxen

These strain the kidneys when you’re dry. Many people with type 1 take one for blood pressure or kidney protection.

Ask your pharmacist to mark your own hold list on paper, before you are ever sick enough to need it. Source: Canadian sick-day medication guidance.

The hold list is not universal. Someone with heart failure may be told to keep a diuretic going. That is why it gets personalised in advance.
