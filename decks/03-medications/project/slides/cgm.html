Medicines that lie to your sensor

Acetaminophen, above the maximum

On Dexcom G6 and G7, normal dosing is fine. Going past the maximum — more than about 1 g every six hours in an adult — can read falsely high.

Hydroxyurea

Reads falsely high on G6 and G7 at any dose. Dexcom says not to use sensor readings for treatment decisions while taking it.

Every brand has its own list

Dexcom, Libre and the others differ. Look up yours rather than assuming this one transfers.

The rule that covers all of it

When the number and the person disagree, believe the person and do a finger prick.

A falsely high reading is the dangerous direction: you correct for glucose that was never there, and the low is real.

Source: Dexcom interference guidance. Older sensor generations were affected by acetaminophen at ordinary doses — newer ones are not.

Worth saying that this is not a reason to avoid acetaminophen. At normal doses on a current sensor it is a non-issue.
