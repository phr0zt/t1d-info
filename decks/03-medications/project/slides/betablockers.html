Beta blockers take away the alarm, not the fire

Your early warning for a low is mostly adrenaline: the shaking, the pounding heart, the sudden anxiety. That is the system telling you to eat something now.

Beta blockers — metoprolol, propranolol, bisoprolol — blunt exactly those signals. The low still happens. The alarm is turned down.

Sweating usually still gets through, because it runs on a different nerve pathway. For some people on a beta blocker, sweating and confusion are the only warnings left.

So if you’re on one

  • Check more often, not less
  • Always check before driving
  • Set your sensor’s low alert higher
  • Tell the people around you that you may not feel it coming
  • Treat an unexplained sweat as a low until proven otherwise

Beta blockers are often the right drug after a heart attack or for blood pressure. This is about managing around them, not avoiding them.

Emphasise the sweating point. It is the one warning people can still trust, and almost nobody is told it survives.
