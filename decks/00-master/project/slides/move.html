Movement goes both ways

Steady effort lowers it

Jogging, cycling, swimming, a long walk. Muscles take glucose out of the blood and injected insulin doesn’t switch off the way a pancreas would. Often within 45 minutes.

Starting range for most: 7 to 10 mmol/L. Under 5, take 10 to 20 g of glucose first.

Hard, short effort raises it

Sprints, heavy lifting, intervals, a match. Adrenaline tells the liver to release glucose faster than muscles use it.

The pre-game high is usually nerves and it settles once play starts. Correcting it hard is what causes the crash later.

Diabetes Canada: 150 minutes a week, spread over at least three days, plus resistance work twice. Don’t train with ketones at or above 1.5, or within 24 hours of a severe low.

The two-directions idea removes most of the confusion people bring to exercise, and with it a lot of the fear that stops them.
