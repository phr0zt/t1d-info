When they can’t swallow: glucagon

The person with diabetes never uses this one themselves — by definition, they can’t. So this slide is for everybody else.

Nasal, 3 mg

A puff into one nostril. No needle, no mixing, and it works even if they are unconscious — they don’t need to breathe it in.

Injectable, 1 mg

Into the thigh, through clothing if you must. Newer pens come ready to use.

Then, every time

Call 911. Roll them on their side — vomiting is common after. Stay until they are properly awake and have eaten. Never put anything in the mouth of someone unconscious.

After drinking, it works less well

Glucagon asks the liver to release glucose, and alcohol stops the liver doing that. Past a couple of drinks, call the ambulance rather than wait.

Everyone on insulin should have one, in date, somewhere the people around them can actually find it. Ask right now whether you know where it is.
