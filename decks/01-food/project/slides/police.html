Don’t be the diabetes police

“Should you be eating that?” is never once helpful. They have insulin. They are dosing for it. They have also heard the question several hundred times.

Hovering and commenting doesn’t improve anyone’s numbers. What it does is make the person hide things — which is how a teenager stops mentioning the lows.

This is a condition managed around every meal, for life. Surveillance wears people down faster than the illness does.

Swap the sentence

“Have you checked?” rather than “you look low”.

“What do you need?” rather than handing them something and hoping.

“I’m serving at seven” rather than “are you allowed this?”

“There’s juice in the fridge” rather than nothing at all.

Knowing where the fast sugar is kept is worth more than any amount of watching what goes on the plate.

If caretakers remember one slide from this deck, make it this one. Good intentions cause most of what is listed here.
