When the insulin goes in matters as much as how much

Insulin injected under the skin takes time to start working. Food does not wait for it. Giving the dose a little ahead of the meal lets the two line up.

Rapid-acting insulin

Diabetes Canada: within 15 minutes of eating. Studies of dosing 15 to 20 minutes ahead show a lower peak afterwards, most noticeably at breakfast.

Faster-acting insulin

Designed to go in right at the meal — zero to two minutes before — and can be given up to 20 minutes after if needed.

When not to dose early

A restaurant that hasn’t served yet. A small child who may not finish. Anyone who might not eat what was planned.

Insulin already working with no food arriving is a low.

Which insulin you have decides the timing, so there is no universal number. Sources: Diabetes Canada · Diabète Québec · ISPAD 2022.

Note that pre-meal dosing did not increase lows in the studies, contrary to what most parents assume. The exception is genuine uncertainty about whether the food will arrive or be eaten.
