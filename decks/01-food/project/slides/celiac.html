Autoimmune conditions travel together

~6%

of people with type 1 also have celiac disease — studies range from about 2% to 16%, against under 1% in the general population.

Autoimmune thyroid disease is even more common, affecting roughly 17 to 30%.

Type 1 is an autoimmune condition, and autoimmune conditions cluster. Screening for celiac and thyroid is routine follow-up — not a sign that something has gone wrong.

Most celiac in type 1 is silent. Only about one in ten people get the classic digestive symptoms; the rest have vague fatigue, brain fog, headaches or joint pain, or nothing at all. It also becomes more common with age, not less.

Guidelines suggest screening soon after diagnosis, again within two years, and again at five — which is why the blood test keeps reappearing.

Sources: ADA Standards of Care 2025 · Breakthrough T1D

Families often read repeat screening as suspicion that something is wrong. Framing it as scheduled and normal removes that.
