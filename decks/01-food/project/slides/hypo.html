The one piece of food knowledge everyone needs

Below 3.9 mmol/L, Diabetes Canada says take 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate — preferably glucose or sucrose as tablets or a solution, which work better than juice or gels.

15 grams is

  • 4 glucose tablets
  • 15 mL (3 tsp) sugar in water, or 3 packets
  • 5 sugar cubes
  • 150 mL juice or regular soft drink
  • 6 Life Savers
  • 15 mL (1 tbsp) honey

Then

Wait 15 minutes, sitting down. Check again. Still under 3.9? Do it again.

Once you’re back up, have the meal that was due. If it’s more than an hour away, take a snack with 15 g of carbohydrate plus some protein.

Does not work

  • Chocolate, biscuits, cake — fat slows the sugar
  • Milk, for the same reason
  • Diet or zero drinks — no sugar in them at all
  • Glucose gel is slow; tablets beat it

In practice many people need more — one Canadian study found an average of 32 g. Start at 15, recheck, and set your own amount with your team. Source: Diabetes Canada.

The diet drink mix-up is the one that catches hosts, where both cans sit in the same fridge and look identical.
