# Research note — 07 Massage, orthotherapy and type 1 diabetes

Compiled 2026-10-03 for deck `decks/07-manual-therapy` and site topic `manual-therapy`
(`therapies-manuelles`). Starting point was a circulating AI-style paragraph on
orthotherapy and T1D; this note records what survived checking and what didn't.

## Fact-check of the starting paragraph

| Claim in the draft | Verdict | What we say instead |
|---|---|---|
| Massage relaxation lowers cortisol/adrenaline, making glucose easier to manage | **Disputed / contradicted.** Moyer 2004 (37 RCTs): single sessions lowered anxiety, BP, HR — not cortisol. Moyer 2011: cortisol effect "not statistically distinguishable from zero" (d 0.05–0.30). Field 2005 (−31 %) used within-group changes. Stress moves glucose inconsistently in T1D (Wiesli 2005, 2007; Franc 2023). | Massage may ease anxiety; it has not been shown to reliably lower stress hormones, and stress moves blood sugar differently in different people. |
| Improves circulation, supports tissue oxygenation | **Weak / unsupported** for lasting benefit. One T2D PAD trial (Castro-Sánchez 2011); immediate-only skin flow (Chatchawan 2020). Linde 1986 measured no change in subcutaneous blood flow. No guideline lists massage for complications. | No good evidence that massage prevents or treats complications. |
| Joint stiffness, frozen shoulder, stiff hands via AGEs | **Supported.** DCCT/EDIC (Larkin 2014, n=1,217, mean 31 y duration): any hand/shoulder condition 66 %; frozen shoulder 31 %, carpal tunnel 30 %, trigger finger 28 %, prayer sign 22 %, Dupuytren 9 %. Associated with skin intrinsic fluorescence (AGE marker). Diabetes ≈5× odds of frozen shoulder (Zreik 2016). | Keep; AGEs as leading explanation, not proven cause. Pair rate with caveat: cohort diagnosed decades ago. |
| Gentle mobilization preserves range of motion | **Partly.** Alsubheen 2019 (8 RCTs, diabetes): mobilization + exercise largest effect, low-quality evidence. Outcomes slower with diabetes (Tang & Tan 2024). | Mobilization + exercise may help; evidence low quality; get a diagnosis first. |
| Massage near a recent injection speeds absorption → low | **Supported** (older insulins). Linde 1986: ~6× elimination rate, n=9. Linde & Philip 1989, Dillon 1983 (glucose −8.3 %), Berger 1982. Sauna +110 %, glucose 3.0–3.3 mmol/L lower (Koivisto 1980). Leg exercise +135 % (Koivisto & Felig 1978). Analogues/pumps: heat data only (Raz 2009, Landau 2014). FIT Canada 2020 §5.2 advises against massaging injection sites. | Keep, with the caveat that all massage studies are small and used older insulins. |
| Check glucose before and after; carbs within reach | **Reasonable precaution**, no direct study. FQM (2007) suggests checking before/after first 2–3 sessions; Diabète Québec (2014) warns of unexpected drops. | Keep, labelled as precaution. |
| Tell the therapist where CGM/pump are | **Supported** and under-stated. Pressure-induced sensor attenuation ("compression lows": Mensh 2013). Dexcom G7 and Abbott Libre 2 manuals: no lotion near sensor. Oils on pump sets = extension of those warnings (not a manufacturer statement). | Add compression lows and oils. |
| Neuropathy: lighter pressure | **Supported** (precaution); add no heat on numb skin (Diabetes Canada foot care: test water by hand). Thai foot massage RCT in T2D (Chatchawan 2015, n=60) showed balance/sensation gains. | Keep, add heat and post-session foot check. |
| Open wounds/slow-healing sores "common complications" | **Overstated.** Avoid wounds — yes; "common" — no. | Avoid wounds/infection in the treatment area. |
| Absolute contraindications: severe hyper, DKA, severe hypo | **Clinically sound, not a sourced list.** FQM 2018: uncontrolled diabetes, hypo episodes, ulcers/wounds. | Phrase as "reschedule" with ketones, recent severe low, illness, wounds. |
| Consult endocrinologist before booking; choose a "certified" orthotherapist | **Over-medicalised / misleading in Québec.** Most people with T1D need no clearance (FQM: ask the doctor "en cas de doute"). Titles are not protected; no order exists. | Tell your team if you have complications or doubts; check association membership. |

## Not in the draft, added

- **Québec regulation:** massothérapie and orthothérapie are not regulated (no order; government declined in 2018). Titles not protected. Associations with directories: FQM, Mon Réseau Plus (RMPQ), RITMA. Physiotherapy = OPPQ. Kinesiology/osteopathy integration into orders announced 16 June 2026, not in force.
- **Coverage:** group insurers require receipts from a member in good standing of a recognized association (Desjardins guide); some need a medical recommendation. CRA authorized-practitioner list (Jan 2026) recognizes massage therapists in BC, NB, NL, ON, PE — **not QC**. Québec credit: generally not eligible (secondary sources citing IN-130; Revenu Québec not directly verified).
- **Lipohypertrophy:** pooled prevalence 34 % in T1D (Deng 2018, 26 studies); therapists may notice lumps first.
- **Orthostatic light-headedness** with autonomic neuropathy: sit before standing.
- **Kinésithérapie** in Québec ≠ French masseur-kinésithérapeute.

## Not verified (do not state as fact)

Field 1997 numbers (original not accessed); Ghazavi 2008 A1C figures; Werner's textbook wording; any manufacturer statement on oils and pump infusion sets; explicit "compression low" wording in official device manuals; first-person reports of compression lows on a massage table.
