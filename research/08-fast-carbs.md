# Research note: 08 Fast-acting carbs

Compiled 2026-10-04 for deck `decks/08-fast-carbs` and site topic `fast-carbs`
(`sucres-rapides`). The starting point was Beyond Type 1's "The Best Fast-Acting Carbs to Treat Low
Blood Sugar" (Ginger Vieira, 3 March 2026, beyondtype1.org/fast-acting-carbs-chart/), a US
chart with no named medical reviewer. This note records which of its rows hold up in Canada and which
do not.

## The Beyond Type 1 chart, row by row

| BT1 row | Verdict for Canada | What we say instead |
|---|---|---|
| Dex4 glucose tabs: 4 = 16 g | **Holds.** 4 g per tablet on the Canadian AMG Medical product; Diabète Québec lists "4 comprimés de Dex4". | Keep. First choice. |
| White sugar: 4 tsp = 16 g | **Overstated.** Canadian Nutrient File (CNF 4318): 5 mL = 4.2 g, so 15 mL ≈ 13 g. Diabetes Canada counts 15 mL (3 tsp) as 15 g. | 15 mL (1 tbsp) in water, "about 13–15 g". Show both. |
| Sugar packets: 4 = 16 g | **Close.** Canadian Redpath sachets are 3.5 g each, so 4 = 14 g. Diabète Québec says 4 sachets; Diabetes Canada says 3. | 4 packets ≈ 14 g. |
| Maple syrup: 1 tbsp = 13 g | **Holds.** CNF 6175: 15 mL = 14.0 g, about 98 % sucrose. Diabète Québec lists it. | Keep. A Québec pantry staple. |
| Honey: 1 tbsp = 17 g | **Holds.** CNF 4294: 15 mL = 17.7 g, with 41 % fructose. | Keep, and note that it is slower than glucose. |
| Juice or soda: ½ cup / 4 oz = 15 g | **Holds by weight** (cola 11.0 g/100 mL, orange 12.1, apple 11.8: CNF). Diabetes Canada uses 150 mL; Diabète Québec uses 150 mL (⅔ cup). Juice is slower, because fructose-based treatment is less effective (ISPAD 2022) and Diabetes Canada calls orange juice slower. | 150 mL regular pop or juice. Pop beats juice. |
| Skittles: 15 = 15 g | **Holds** (US label ≈ 0.96 g each). Contains hydrogenated palm kernel oil (1 g fat per 28 g). | Keep. "A little fat." |

| Smarties: 2 rolls = 12 g | **Wrong product in Canada.** Canadian Smarties are Nestlé chocolate. The US dextrose candy is sold here as **Rockets** (smarties.com FAQ). | Jelly Belly: 15 = 15 g | **Holds** (≈1.06 g per bean: 35 = 37 g). | 15 beans ≈ 16 g. |
| Brach's jelly beans: 7 = 15 g | US brand, not checked on a Canadian label. | Left out; covered by "make your own row". |
| Welch's fruit gummies 0.8 oz = 17 g | US pouch size; Canadian pouches not checked. | Left out. |
| Sour Patch Kids: 5 = 15 g (image) / 6 = 14 g (web text) | **BT1 disagrees with itself, and the Canadian label differs.** Mondelez Canada: 16 pieces = 36 g → ≈2.2 g each, so 5 ≈ 11 g. | 7 pieces ≈ 15 g (Canadian label). |
| Large marshmallows: 2 = 12 g | Under 15 g (Jet-Puffed ≈ 6 g each, US label). | Left out. |

## What the BT1 page doesn't say, added here

- **Speed numbers (Diabetes Canada 2018, Ch. 14):** 15 g glucose raises blood glucose about 2.1 mmol/L within 20 minutes; 20 g raises it about 3.6 mmol/L at 45 minutes. Glucose gel is "quite slow" (<1.0 mmol/L at 20 minutes). Glucose or sucrose as tablets or solution is "preferable to orange juice and glucose gels".
- **20 g when someone needs help but can swallow** (Diabetes Canada: severe hypoglycemia in a conscious adult). Diabète Québec's 20 g list: 5 Dex4, 20 mL syrup, 200 mL (¾ cup) pop or juice, 8 Life Savers, 3 rolls of Rockets.
- **Children are dosed by age, not by weight (Diabetes Canada 2018, Ch. 34, Table 2):** <5 years 5 g (1 glucose tablet, 40 mL juice); 5–10 years 10 g (2–3 tablets, 85 mL); >10 years 15 g (4 tablets, 125 mL). ISPAD 2022 uses 0.3 g/kg instead (≈9 g at 30 kg, 15 g over 50 kg; grade C). Adult Table 4 says explicitly that it does not apply to children.
- **Automated insulin delivery:** ISPAD 2022: "consideration should be given to treat hypoglycemia with less glucose (e.g., 5 to 10 grams)" (grade E, expert opinion). EASD/ADA AID consensus 2022 and Sherr 2023 say fewer carbs, with no number. Insulet (Omnipod 5) says "some people find they need less". Diabetes Canada 2018 predates this and does not mention it. Tandem and Medtronic statements were not found.
- **CGM lag:** ADA Safe at School (2023): CGM may lag a meter by 5–15 minutes; use a fingerstick before treating a second time. ISPAD 2022: don't repeat treatment on a low sensor reading at 15 minutes without a finger-prick check. Diabète Québec: confirm by finger prick when the CGM doesn't match symptoms.
- **Fructose:** ISPAD 2022 cites Husband et al.: fructose-based treatment (fruit juice) was less effective than glucose and sucrose. Jelly beans resolve lows more slowly. Fat (chocolate) should be avoided as first treatment. No Canadian or ADA statement on agave was found, so we say nothing specific about it.
- **Thresholds:** Diabetes Canada 2018 defines hypoglycemia as <4.0 mmol/L and re-treats if still <4.0. Diabète Québec and the CGM consensus use <3.9. We write "under 4.0 (3.9 on many meters and reports)".
- **"Treat it like medicine"** is BT1's best idea and stays in, framed as a tool rather than a judgment about overeating.

## Not verified (do not state as fact)

Canadian labels for Skittles, Starburst, Life Savers and Jelly Belly (all US labels); Mott's Fruitsations and Welch's pouches in Canada; Canadian Smarties carbohydrate content (irrelevant, since chocolate is excluded on fat grounds); sugar-cube weight in Canada (USDA 2.3 g, so 5 cubes may be ≈12 g; we cite Diabetes Canada's "5 cubes" and say cube sizes vary).
